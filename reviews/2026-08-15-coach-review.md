- **Coach:** Nightmvre — first session watching this team
- **Source:** 13-minute voice review, recorded and transcribed automatically

A note on names: feedback below is written by **role** rather than by person, so it reads as
team process instead of a list of who messed up. The specifics are all still here — if you'd

## The three that actually matter

Everything else on this page is downstream of these.

1. **Our champion pools don't match what we can actually play.** The coach drafts off our
   S/A/B/C tiers. Several are wrong, so the drafts are being built on bad information.
2. **Calls are going unanswered.** Not overruled — *unanswered*. That's the single most
   fixable thing here and it costs us objectives.
3. **We're playing like five solo players who happen to share a lobby.** The coach's words:
   it looked like a five-stack versus solo-queuers, and we were the solo-queuers.

---

## 1. Champion pools are lying to the draft

**What happened.** Asked about a champion listed as **S tier**, the player said they weren't
comfortable on it — "I just think it's good right now." The coach's response: *"Don't put it
as S tier if you can't play it, because I'm going off these."*

**Why this is the top item.** The tier list isn't a meta ranking. It's the input the coach
uses to decide what we draft. When an S means "strong patch pick" for one person and "I'm
confident on this" for another, the draft is assembled from a mix of the two — and nobody
finds out until someone is standing in a lane on a champion they can't play.

**What to do**

- Re-audit every pool this week. One rule: **the tier is how well *you* play it, not how
  good it is right now.** If you wouldn't want it in a tournament game, it isn't S.
- If you can't play it, take it off the list. A short honest pool drafts better than a long
  aspirational one.
- Meta picks you want to *learn* are worth tracking — just not in the same list the coach
  drafts from.
- **Support specifically:** our support pool is almost entirely engage. That constrains
  every comp around it, because the whole team can't be engage — someone has to be able to
  disengage. Two or three enchanters you can actually play would open up the draft a lot.
  Noted that this isn't a skill gap so much as a preference one; it's still a real limit.

## 2. Answer the call, even when the answer is no

**What happened.** The top laner asked for Dragon — at a genuinely good moment, with the
enemy jungler top-side. No response. Not a "no", not a "wait". Silence, repeatedly.

**The coach's point, precisely:** the problem wasn't whether the call was right. It was that
*a reasonable call got no reply at all.* "No, I need to back, I have 2k gold" would have been
a completely fine answer.

**What to do**

- **Every call gets a verbal response.** Yes / no / wait — with a reason if there is one.
- A one-word answer is enough. The failure mode isn't disagreement, it's silence.
- This also defuses the "multiple people shot calling" problem without needing a rule about
  who calls: if calls get answered, they resolve instead of stacking up.

## 3. Team habits, not solo-queue habits

**What the coach saw.** People treating teammates the way you treat randoms in solo queue —
"this is a random guy, why would I entertain that." Understandable when we haven't played
much together, and still the thing that loses tournament games.

**Useful context from the coach:** players who perform in tournaments usually beat their solo
rank, because they're scrimming constantly against better players and playing as a team. Rank
gaps explain some of the gap we felt, but not all of it. Reps together explain the rest.

**What to do**

- More volume as a five. This is mostly a time problem, not a talent problem.
- Say it out loud in the lobby: this isn't solo queue. The coach flagged that it needs to be
  addressed directly rather than assumed.

## 4. Hard CC without follow-up (the Thresh / Amumu game)

That comp is a pick comp. Play slow, look for a hook or an ult, and **collapse the moment it
lands** — hard CC is very difficult to escape, and we had the damage to punish it.

**What went wrong:** Thresh and Amumu drifted too far from the group. They'd engage, and no
damage followed — or Amumu would chase a kill with nobody behind him.

**What to do**

- CC engages only from inside range of our damage. If the damage can't reach it, it isn't a
  pick, it's a 1v5.
- When the hook or ult lands, everyone commits. That's the entire win condition of the comp.

---

## 5. Overstaying, and calling the reset

Thresh repeatedly overstayed hunting one more pick — enemy team at full HP, our damage
already walked out. Nothing was available and we lost tempo or a body for it.

**What to do**

- Someone calls the reset out loud: *"we're leaving,"* *"back out."*
- Cut losses early. The coach specifically wanted more awareness of **when we reset**, not
  just whether we win the fight.

---

## 6. Tunnelling on kills that were never there

The pattern: decide someone is dead, chase, fail to catch or kill them, and turn around with
no cooldowns as their whole team arrives.

There's a clean example from our side — a fight was called off, the team took it anyway, and
the caller got run down while two people fought separately. Both halves of that are the same
problem: we committed without agreeing to commit.

**What to do**

- Know the win condition of *our* comp before the fight starts, and don't step outside it.
- If a chase hasn't worked in three seconds, it isn't working.

---

## 7. Lane priority has to be executed (game 2)

The coach called for early top pressure so Mundo could get items and survive to six. It
didn't happen — top was treated as weak side by default, and Renekton was left to snowball
and dive repeatedly. Mundo had no path back into the game.

**What to do**

- When a lane priority is called pre-game, it's a plan, not a suggestion. Get the gank, or
  say early that it isn't happening so the plan can change.
- Weak-siding a lane is a decision we make deliberately, not a default.
- Mid was genuinely stuck here — an oppressive enemy mid meant shoving and roaming wasn't
  free. That's a real constraint, and it means the help had to come from jungle.

---

## 8. Bot lane positioning (the Zilean / Tristana game)

Karma kept walking up into a lane that could stun-and-all-in her. With Ezreal outranging
Tristana and Karma's shields available, the lane just needed to **play back** and let the
range advantage work.

**What to do**

- Against hard CC plus an all-in threat, respect the setup. Ego-checking a Zilean stun is a
  free kill for them.
- Play the advantage we actually have — in that lane it was range, not aggression.

## What we agreed to do next

Straight from the end of the call:

1. **Lock our champion pools down** so they line up with what we actually play.
2. **Fix the communication gaps** so we stop missing objectives that were there.
3. **Identify our win conditions better**, so we stop overstepping what the comp can do and
   putting ourselves in worse positions instead of playing to our strengths.
4. **Get more games in together** — the fix for most of the above is reps.
